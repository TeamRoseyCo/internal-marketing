## Technical Decisions

### Decision 1: Local Relevance Over Domain Authority

**Choice:** Prioritize Belfast-relevant links (DA 40-50) over generic high-DA links (DA 80+)

**Rationale:**
- Google local ranking algorithm weighs local relevance heavily
- Belfast Chamber link (DA ~45) signals community integration better than generic directory (DA 80)
- Research shows locally-relevant links provide stronger local ranking boost
- Supports topical authority strategy from Phase 10 (Belfast-focused content)

**Alternatives considered:**
- Generic high-DA link building (rejected: less effective for local rankings)
- Volume-based directory submissions (rejected: outdated 2020 approach)

**Impact:** Link building strategy emphasizes Belfast sources first, then expands to NI-wide and UK sources

---

### Decision 2: Quality-First Citation Strategy (Tier-Based)

**Choice:** Build 14 Tier 1 foundational citations, then 20-25 Tier 2 secondary sources (stop there)

**Rationale:**
- Research shows first 14 citations provide 60-70% of ranking benefit
- Next 20-25 provide 30-40% of remaining benefit
- Beyond ~40 citations, diminishing returns
- Tier approach prevents wasting time on low-value directories
- Ensures NAP consistency across all listings (manageable number)

**Alternatives considered:**
- Mass submission to 100+ directories (rejected: time-intensive, minimal benefit in 2026)
- Only GBP optimization without citations (rejected: leaves ranking opportunity on table)

**Impact:** Citation strategy focuses on quality and NAP consistency over volume

---

### Decision 3: HARO as Primary Media Link Channel

**Choice:** Daily HARO query monitoring as passive media link building tactic

**Rationale:**
- HARO revived April 2025 (was shut down, now back)
- Provides access to journalist queries from high-DR publications
- 10-20% acceptance rate for well-matched queries
- Each accepted pitch = high-DR (70-95) editorial backlink
- Free tier sufficient for initial usage
- Passive approach (respond to inbound queries vs. cold pitching)

**Alternatives considered:**
- Cold pitching journalists without HARO (rejected: 1% response rate vs. 10-20% for HARO)
- Paid PR distribution services (rejected: expensive, less targeted)

**Implementation notes:**
- Explicit warning about AI detection (HARO bans AI-generated responses)
- Templates emphasize personalization and genuine expertise
- 2-hour response window for highest acceptance rates

---

### Decision 4: Relationship-Based Partnership Outreach

**Choice:** Target 3-5 genuine business partnerships vs. mass reciprocal link exchanges

**Rationale:**
- Small number of quality partnerships > many transactional link swaps
- Google flags excessive reciprocal linking (A↔B repeatedly)
- Genuine partnerships provide ongoing value (referrals, content collaboration, link opportunities)
- Belfast business relationships strengthen local authority signals
- Value-first approach (give before asking) builds trust

**Alternatives considered:**
- Mass reciprocal link exchanges (rejected: penalized by Google)
- Generic partner directories (rejected: low relevance, minimal SEO value)

**Impact:** Partnership templates emphasize specific collaboration proposals and mutual benefit

---

### Decision 5: Budget-Conscious Phased Approach

**Choice:** Start with free tools and opportunities, invest in paid tools/memberships after 2-3 clients

**Rationale:**
- Aligns with Phase 6 decision to minimize upfront costs before client revenue
- HARO, Google Search Console, manual tracking are free and sufficient initially
- Belfast Chamber membership (£200-500/year) provides high ROI but delayed until budget confirmed
- BrightLocal (£39/month) nice-to-have but can use manual tracking first
- Allows testing link building approaches before committing to paid tools

**Budget breakdown:**
- Phase 1 (first 2-3 clients): £200-500 total (minimal paid sponsorships)
- Phase 2 (after 2-3 clients): £500-1000 total (add Chamber membership, citation tools)

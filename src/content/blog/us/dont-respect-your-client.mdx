One time, I went to the doctor because I was sick.

Picture a doctor in your head. Now this guy strolls in wearing a hoodie, overweight, zero energy. He spoke like he didn't care whether I lived or died — like he was forced to be there.

And then it hit me: **I wasn't just judging his advice. I was judging him.** His clothes, his shape, his attitude.

I still hate that doctor, by the way. Not because of what he prescribed. Because of how he carried himself.

## Here's the lesson

Your client is doing the same thing to *you*, every single moment.

The way you show up — your website, your DMs, your invoice, your voicemail, the photo you use on LinkedIn — they're all under the microscope. People decide whether to trust you with their money in the first 3 seconds. And they decide it based on signals you might not even be paying attention to.

It's not about being fake. It's about not being sloppy.

## Specifically:

- **Your website**: looks built in 2014? You look like a 2014 business.
- **Your social posts**: low effort? You look like a low-effort operator.
- **Your reply time**: 4 days? You look like you don't care.
- **Your offer**: vague? You look like you don't know what you do.

Each of those is a small signal. Stack 5 of them and you've already lost the sale before you ever got on a call.

## So what do I mean by "don't respect your client"?

I mean: don't assume they'll forgive the small stuff because you're "good at the actual work." They won't. They'll quietly book your competitor who *looked* the part — even if your work is better.

Dress the way you want to be treated. **Build the brand you want to be paid like.**
